नमस्ते राही! *Hello, traveller.* I'm Aditi, and you've wandered into my small cabinet of curiosities on the web.

## At work

I convince computers to understand human intent. I'm an LLVM compiler engineer at IBM, working on PowerPC, and before that I spent more than two years at AMD on the AMDGPU backend. Most of my work sits where a compiler turns what you meant into instructions a machine can actually run: new instructions, smarter lowering and stricter checks, all upstream and in the open.

I enjoy solving problems where software meets silicon. I believe elegant engineering is invisible: the best systems quietly do their job. My corner of computing is LLVM, compilers, programming languages, computer architecture and high-performance computing, and lately I'm curious about AI infrastructure, thoughtful product design and the future of computing.

I'm **open to work**, so if you're building something in compilers, toolchains or AI infrastructure, I'd love to hear from you.

## Away from the terminal

I collect sunsets, product ideas, travel memories and impossible bugs. Bengaluru keeps me happily busy: you'll often find me at a talk, a reading or an exhibition at BIC, Atta Galatta, Champaca, Sabha or MAP. I cook my way across cuisines, take far too many photos when I travel, and keep a running list of songs and podcasts I can't stop recommending. My headphones still jump from Pachelbel's Canon in D to Lata Mangeshkar and Kishore Kumar classics.

Every week I set myself something new to learn: a few words of a new language, or one more rabbit hole into the brain, biology or why people behave the way they do. On Sundays, it all goes into Dear Diary.

## On this site

When I have something important, clever, funny or slightly silly to say, it ends up here. A few pieces are polished. Most are seedlings, rough drafts still waiting for an editor, and I've made my peace with that. As I wrote on this very site years ago, life is a collection of unorganised, unexpected events, with a few planned ones in between.

Take Route 1 if you're here for the work. If you have a little time, take the long way round.
